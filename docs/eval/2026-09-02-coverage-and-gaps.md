# Coverage, gap ledger and discovery lift, as measured on 2026-09-02

The demand side is simulated on real Comtrade import values under real PIF portfolio names; the supply side is real. Every figure below is regenerated by `bun run numbers` into `docs/numbers.md`; this note explains what the figures mean and what they do not.

## The demand set

96 purchase-request lines under 12 portfolio company names, in the ERP formats each company would use, Arabic and English mixed, with misspellings, mixed units and abbreviations. Each line's annual value is a share of Saudi imports of its subheading (Comtrade 2024) times `PORTFOLIO_IMPORT_SHARE` = 12%. The Coordinator resolved all 96 to an HS subheading and pooled them into 72 orders worth USD 357.9M a year; the largest pooled seven lines from three companies.

## The two coverage figures

**At the stated specification: 0.0% of spend, 0 of 72 orders.** A pooled order counts here only when a supported supplier at its subheading states at least one attribute that agrees with the pooled envelope (material, size, pressure or connection), none that conflicts, and names the object class on a catch-all subheading. Not one order met it. Of 187 supported capabilities in the slice, 8 state any attribute at all, and none of those eight lines up with an order.

**At category level: 98.9% of spend, 70 of 72 orders.** A verified plant declares the order's subheading with nothing in conflict. This is the registry view: Tarmeez says who declares valves, pumps and fittings, the Auditor confirmed those plants are real, and nobody has verified what they can make at specification.

The distance between the two figures is the blind spot the deck describes: self-declared tariff lines with a tonnage and no specification. It is also the Detectives' queue. The 28 orders at HS 848180 (other valves, USD 223.9M a year) are where a specification pass pays first.

## The gap ledger

Two supply gaps, both carbon-steel butt-weld tees (4 inch and 2 inch, USD 4.1M a year together): no supported supplier of any class at those subheadings. The Advisor wrote an investment case for each, naming Eastern Region valve makers with pipe-fitting capability as pivot candidates. No manufacturing gaps: wherever a supported supplier exists at category level, at least one is a manufacturer.

That the ledger holds two gaps is a property of this demand set at category level. Under the at-spec rule the ledger's honest reading is: 70 orders have a declared, unverified supplier; 2 have nobody.

## Discovery lift

The long-tail hunt searched for valve, pump and fitting makers in no registry, found 26 candidates and created 17 suppliers, each investigated by a Detective and audited. Thirteen of them hold at least one supported capability, among them Covna Valves, Arabian Pumps, Lamah and New World Horizon. Registry-only suppliers (MLCP, Made in Saudi) added none in this slice: four mention the sector in their activity text and none makes valves or pumps.

## What the swarm cost

Sixty Detective runs took 93 minutes and 279 audits took 168 minutes on one 16 GB laptop with `qwen3.5:9b`, every call serialised through one local Ollama. The audit path was switched to JSON text mid-run, which halved the average audit from about fifty to about seventeen seconds. Over the whole day `bun run cost` reports, per successful run: Detective 98 s and about USD 0.05 at Claude Opus 5 prices (USD 0.02 at Sonnet 5), Auditor 22 s and about USD 0.01, Coordinator 66 s and about USD 0.02, Advisor 63 s and about USD 0.03. Local marginal cost is electricity.

The Coordinator's 66 s against the 47 s of the milestone-1 spike is the machine, not the model: Ollama runs with `OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS=1`, and every Coordinator call embeds with `bge-m3` before generating with `qwen3.5:9b`, so the two models swap on every line. Allowing two loaded models (both fit in 16 GB) removes the swap. The Detective and Auditor averages also include hours in which tests and recordings competed for the same model.

## Defects found by the first live runs, all fixed under test the same day

- Ollama returned nested tool-call arrays as JSON strings; the Detective silently dropped every capability and evidence record on such runs.
- Findings without an HS guess became orphan capabilities under 000000, which nothing can match; they now anchor to the declared line or a sector subheading.
- Certificates claimed on a supplier's own site or LinkedIn page landed at Tier 1; the host now decides the tier.
- A refuted spec lens killed real products; only a refuted real lens kills now, and a spec refute strips the unsubstantiated attributes.
- The hunt's prose reply was accepted as an empty candidate list; a reply with no JSON object is now a failure that is retried.
- Qwen drops one closing brace before a trailing key; the parser repairs it before giving up.
- A test fixture deleted and reloaded the real Kanoo valves plant on every suite run, wiping its Detective and Auditor results.
